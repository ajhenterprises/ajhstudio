A redesign is a bigger commitment than a routine update, so it's worth being clear about when it's actually necessary. Here are five signs worth paying attention to.

## 1. It doesn't work well on a phone

If a site was built years ago, there's a good chance it wasn't designed mobile-first — and mobile traffic now makes up the majority of visits for most businesses. A site that's hard to navigate, read, or interact with on a phone is turning visitors away before they see what you offer.

## 2. It's slow

Visitors don't wait around for a slow site to load. Neither do search engines, which increasingly factor speed into rankings. If pages take several seconds to load, that's not a minor annoyance — it's actively costing traffic and credibility.

## 3. Updating it requires help every time

If every small change — a new staff photo, an updated hour, a fixed typo — requires reaching out to someone and waiting, the site has become a bottleneck instead of a tool. A redesign is a chance to rebuild on a foundation that's easier to manage.

## 4. It doesn't reflect the business anymore

Businesses evolve. Services change, offerings expand, branding matures. A website that was accurate five years ago can end up quietly out of date — still live, still technically working, but no longer telling the truth about what the business actually does.

## 5. It's hard to tell what to do next

A visitor should always have a clear next step: call, fill out a form, browse services, schedule a visit. If a site is cluttered, confusing, or missing a clear call to action, it's not doing its job — even if it looks fine at a glance.

## When a redesign isn't the answer

Not every problem requires starting over. Sometimes updated copy, a few new photos, or small structural changes solve the real issue without a full rebuild. Part of a good redesign conversation is figuring out which one you actually need.
