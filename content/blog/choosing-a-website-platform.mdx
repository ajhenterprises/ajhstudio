Every website conversation eventually lands on the same question: *what should this be built on?* WordPress, Squarespace, Wix, a custom build — the options multiply fast, and most of the advice online is either outdated or written by someone selling a specific platform.

Here's the more useful way to think about it.

## Start with what the site needs to do

Before any platform conversation, get specific about what the website actually needs to accomplish. A five-page site for a local service business has very different requirements than a blog-driven site for a ministry, or a catalog-style site for a retailer. The platform should follow the requirements — not the other way around.

Ask:

- Who updates the site day to day, and how comfortable are they with technology?
- Does the site need to support a blog, events calendar, or online store?
- How much content is there now, and how much will there be in two years?
- What's the plan for hosting, security, and updates after launch?

## Weigh flexibility against complexity

Every platform is a trade-off between flexibility and simplicity. A fully custom build gives you the most control but requires more ongoing technical support. A page-builder platform is easier to hand off but can feel limiting once a business outgrows it.

Neither option is universally "better." The right choice depends on the size of the organization, the pace of change, and who's responsible for the site once it's live.

## Don't underestimate what happens after launch

A website platform isn't just a design tool — it's the foundation for hosting, security, and updates for years to come. Some platforms bundle hosting and maintenance together. Others require you to piece it together yourself. Understanding that difference up front avoids a lot of frustration later.

> The best platform is the one that fits how your business actually operates — not the one with the longest feature list.

## The short version

There's no single right answer, but there is a right question: what does this site need to do, for whom, and who's going to keep it running? Answer that first, and the platform decision gets a lot simpler.
